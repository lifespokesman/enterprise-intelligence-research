# Five Long-Term Organizational Questions

[简体中文](../README.md) | **English**

This directory maintains long-term organizational questions rather than expanding the framework around every new technology label.

## Q1 | Cognition and Decision Rights

**Question:** When humans and AI have different cognitive advantages, limitations, and responsibility-bearing capacities, how should decision rights be allocated?

Current hypothesis: decision rights may become less purely role-based and increasingly depend on combinations such as:

`Task × Risk × Information Advantage × AI Capability × Human Responsibility`

Key variables include information advantage, judgment reliability, reversibility, feedback speed, value judgment, risk level, and the ability to bear responsibility.

---

## Q2 | Accountability, Risk, and a New Agency Problem

**Question:** When AI can participate in decisions but cannot bear professional, social, or legal consequences in the human sense, where should accountability ultimately reside?

Current hypothesis: part of the AI-era agency problem may shift from traditional **Interest Alignment** toward:

`Intent Alignment + Authority Alignment + Accountability Allocation`

Research objects include Agent Owner, Producer, User, Business Owner, Authority Source, Trace, Evidence, Approval, and Audit.

---

## Q3 | Coordination in Human-Agent Organizations

**Question:** How should humans, agents, software, and enterprise assets form stable patterns of coordination?

Current hypothesis: Shared Context or an Enterprise World Model may become a new form of **semantic coordination infrastructure** for human-agent organizations.

This project does not currently claim that it is a new coordination mechanism that replaces Mintzberg's established mechanisms. It may instead augment hierarchy, workflow, standardization, and mutual adjustment.

Key topics: Hierarchy, Workflow, Standardization, Mutual Adjustment, Shared Semantics, Multi-Agent Coordination.

---

## Q4 | Firm Boundaries and Capability Networks

**Question:** If AI significantly lowers the costs of discovering capabilities, negotiating, coordinating, monitoring, and verifying work, how might this change which capabilities should remain inside the firm and which can be dynamically accessed outside it?

Current hypothesis: some capabilities may become easier to assemble dynamically across organizational boundaries, while asset specificity, security, accountability, strategic control, confidentiality, and long-term investment may continue to favor internalization.

Key theories and concepts: Coase, Williamson, Transaction Cost Economics, Capability Discovery, Dynamic Teams, Firm Boundaries.

---

## Q5 | Organizational Learning and Enterprise Evolution

**Question:** How can the operating experience of AI agents become durable organizational capability rather than disappear inside isolated sessions?

Current hypothesis: traces, evidence, outcomes, failures, and human feedback only become organizational learning when they are transformed into durable and governable assets such as Knowledge, Skills, Rules, World Models, Evaluation Sets, Policies, and Organizational Memory.

Key theories and concepts: March, Argyris & Schön, Exploration vs. Exploitation, Organizational Memory, Agent Learning.

---

These questions can support later work on intelligent-subject relationships, engineering, and governance. They are not intended to map one-to-one onto technical architecture layers.
