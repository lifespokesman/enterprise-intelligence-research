# Topic | What Is Enterprise Intelligence?

[简体中文](../enterprise-intelligence.md) | **English**

## 0. Core Question

Before discussing models, agents, harnesses, runtimes, ontologies, or capabilities, what kind of **intelligence** does an enterprise actually need?

## Working Definition v0.1

> **Enterprise intelligence is an organizational capability: the ability of an organization to configure cognition, decision rights, action capacity, and learning under goals, resource constraints, institutional rules, and risk boundaries, so that it can continuously sense its environment, interpret reality, form judgments, organize capabilities, act, and adapt from feedback.**

A simplified loop:

`Sense → Understand → Judge → Organize → Act → Feedback → Learn`

## Three Levels of Intelligence

### Model Intelligence

The reasoning, prediction, interpretation, and generation capabilities of a model.

### Subject Intelligence

The ability of an intelligent subject to pursue goals, maintain context, make judgments, act, and adapt through feedback over time.

### Enterprise Intelligence

The organizational capability that emerges from how humans, AI, software, data, and physical or digital assets are configured into a system of cognition, decision, action, and learning.

## Current Judgment

Enterprise intelligence did not begin with AI.

Long before modern AI, organizational intelligence was distributed across:

- human expertise and judgment;
- division of labor;
- institutions, policies, and rules;
- business processes;
- software systems;
- data and reporting;
- meetings and coordination mechanisms.

The important change introduced by AI is that parts of enterprise intelligence that previously depended heavily on tacit human cognition, fixed workflows, and manual coordination can increasingly become software-mediated, scalable, and continuously executable.

This does **not** imply that more AI automatically produces a more intelligent organization.

> **Enterprise intelligence is an organizational property, not an additive property of individual AI agents.**

A firm may deploy highly capable agents and still perform poorly as an intelligent organization if semantics are fragmented, decision rights conflict, coordination breaks down, accountability is unclear, or experience is not converted into organizational learning.

## Five Capability Questions Behind Enterprise Intelligence

Enterprise intelligence currently appears to involve at least five capabilities:

1. **Situational sensing** — What is happening?
2. **Business interpretation** — What does it mean in this organizational context?
3. **Judgment and decision** — What should be done?
4. **Organization and action** — How can the enterprise actually mobilize capabilities and change the world?
5. **Learning and adaptation** — Can the organization become better after observing the result?

The deeper organizational question is not simply whether AI can perform these activities, but **who or what should perform them, under what authority, with what coordination mechanisms, and how the resulting experience becomes organizational capability**.

## What This Topic Is Not Claiming

- Enterprise intelligence is **not** equivalent to model capability.
- Enterprise intelligence is **not** the number of deployed agents.
- Enterprise intelligence is **not** the same as automation rate.
- Giving AI more autonomy does not necessarily make the organization more intelligent.
- Local intelligence gains can create new organizational complexity if decision, action, accountability, and learning fail to form a closed loop.

## Open Research Direction

A long-term question of this project is whether AI-native organizations will increasingly develop a continuous loop involving:

**Intelligent Subjects → Shared Understanding of the Enterprise World → Capability Organization → Action → Real-world Feedback → Organizational Learning**

The implementation may involve agents, ontologies, knowledge systems, APIs, workflows, or future technologies, but these implementation choices should not be confused with the underlying organizational problem.
